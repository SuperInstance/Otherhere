# Vision: Otherhere, fully grown

_Not a roadmap. A picture of what people could do with Otherhere when every part of it is mature: what it is for, told from the people using it. Use it to check whether a decision moves toward this or away from it._

The brief says the world "remembers like a person does: vivid where you are now, the essence of where you've been, and specific memories that never fade." Taken all the way, that is not a map or a game engine. It is a shared, living memory of the Earth, sketched everywhere and sharpened by love and attention, that belongs to the people who made it.

## Ten people, some years from now

**Ruth, 81, in Sitka.** Her grandson films a slow walk down Lincoln Street on his phone. That evening her World shows the street sharper than she has seen it on any screen: St. Michael's onion dome, the green of the bank, the rain on the harbour. She asks for the street as it was in 1962. Captures and archive photos that neighbours have shared over the years let it settle back, building by building: the cannery, the old boardwalk, her father's boat at the float. Where nobody remembered, it stays a soft pencil sketch, and she can tell the difference. That honesty is why she trusts the rest. She narrates a story over it and saves it as a Memory, private, for the family.

**Amara, 9, in Lagos.** At school she builds a treasure hunt in her own neighbourhood by talking to it: "the clue is under the blue water tank, and if you're slow, the market closes." The Ruleset compiles from her words into data, never code, so nothing she makes can break anyone's world. Her cousins in Houston play it that night on her street, which they have never seen, and they learn which way the mosque faces and where the best puff-puff is sold. Next year her whole class maps their streets for a city-wide game, and the city gets the most detailed model of its informal settlements it has ever had. The children's names stay private.

**Tomás, 34, emigrated twenty years ago.** He walks his childhood street in Valparaíso from an apartment in Rotterdam. The colours on the hillside houses are right, because a hundred strangers' photos settled them. One house is still a sketch: his own. He asks his mother to film it on her next visit. Weeks later it sharpens in front of him, down to the peeling yellow paint, and he cries a little. He sends her the place as a Capsule, scoped to the two of them.

**A volunteer team, the morning after a flood.** Responders walk a river town with phones and a drone. Each capture becomes Facts with provenance and confidence: which bridge is out, which roads are passable, where the waterline reached. Coordinators far away see a world that is honest about what it knows and when it learned it. Fresh Facts are crisp, stale ones fade, and guesses look like guesses. Because Plugins pull work rather than receive it, a laptop in the school gym does the heavy lifting with no internet beyond a satellite link.

**Ines, a historian.** She layers time on a single square in Kraków: 1890, 1939, 1945, 1989, today. Every layer is Facts with sources, and archival sources rank above imagination. Students walk through the decades and watch the square change. Each building tells them how it knows what it shows. Her published Capsule becomes a citable, versioned scholarly object, because a Bundle is a pure function of its Facts.

**Daniel, who is blind.** The world he walks has no pixels. It has sound: the slope of the street underfoot, the echo of an arcade, the harbour bell ahead and to the left, a narrated description generated from the same Facts that draw the buildings for everyone else. He plans a route through a city he will visit next week, learns it by walking it at home, and arrives already knowing the way.

**Two friends, Seoul and Nairobi, Friday night.** Their match is capture the flag across a real campus that neither has visited. The terrain is the real hill, and the cover is the real hedges. Team fog hides what your side hasn't seen. Afterwards they keep the replay as a Capsule. It rebuilds identically years later because everything in it is content-addressed.

**Mei, a painter.** She works in a world that renders in her own style. Her brush strokes trained a style that reads the same Facts as everyone else's, so her Hangzhou looks like her paintings and still has every alley in its true place. Her style itself is a shareable, versioned Capsule. Other people walk their own towns in it and send her their favourite views.

**A biologist in the Tongass.** Volunteers photograph trees on their hikes. Species, height and canopy become Facts, and the Town's Essence slowly turns from a regional guess into the forest's real composition. Imagined hemlock gives way to observed hemlock. Over ten years the World holds a careful record of a forest as it changes: visible, walkable, and owned by the people who recorded it.

**Anyone, anywhere, on their first visit.** They type a street address in a town nobody has played yet. Within seconds the place exists: real ground, real coastline, real streets from OpenStreetMap, imagined buildings that already look like that region rather than like nowhere, soft until someone looks closer. The whole Earth is already a sketch, waiting for attention.

## What makes it possible (and what must never be traded away)

- **Facts with provenance, not pixels.** Every one of these stories depends on the world knowing how it knows things, and showing it.
- **Derived = f(Facts).** Replays, citations, styles and time travel all work because what you see can always be rebuilt from what is known.
- **Private by default, shared by choice.** Ruth's Memory, Amara's name and Tomás's house belong to them. Trust is the platform.
- **Imagination that admits it is imagining.** The sketch is a feature. A world that pretends to know everything cannot be trusted with anything.
- **No single centre.** People own their Worlds. Plugins lend compute, and the edge coordinates without owning.
- **Models as instruments, not authorities.** Cheap models describe and propose. Facts are decided by rules, ranks and the people who were there. Model output is data, never code.

## North-star questions for any change

1. Does this make the world more honest about what it knows?
2. Does it let one more kind of person bring their place in?
3. Could it be rebuilt, byte for byte, from the Facts in ten years?
4. Whose is it, and did they choose to share it?
5. Is the cheapest correct path also the default path, so it works on an old phone in a place with bad signal?
